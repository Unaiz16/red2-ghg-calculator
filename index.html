How to read the result

Bio content is not GHG savings

Bio content says where the carbon in a fuel comes from. GHG savings say how much less greenhouse gas the whole supply chain emits per megajoule than the fossil fuel comparator of 94 gCO2eq/MJ. These are two different questions.

Growing or collecting the feedstock, processing it and transporting the fuel all cause emissions. So a fuel that is 99% bio by energy, made at 20 gCO2eq/MJ and blended with 1% fossil, comes out at 20.74 gCO2eq/MJ and saves 77.9%, not 99%. The blend mode above starts with exactly this example.

The feedstock matters most. With Annex V default values, biodiesel from waste cooking oil saves 84%, while biodiesel from rape seed saves 47%, mainly because a waste carries no cultivation emissions.

E = eec + el + ep + etd + eu − esca − eccs − eccr
Savings = (94 − E) / 94

Annex V Part C point 1(a) and point 19 (fossil fuel comparator EF(t) = 94 gCO2eq/MJ).

What each term means

E-fuels: why grid electricity can fail the 70% minimum

E-fuels here means renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuels of non-biological origin (RFNBO), such as hydrogen from electrolysis and fuels made from it, and recycled carbon fuels. Both use the same formula from Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1185 (Annex Part A point 1) and the same fossil fuel comparator of 94 gCO2eq/MJ (Part A point 2). The minimum saving is 70%: for RFNBO under RED Art. 29a(1) (inserted by RED III; previously Art. 25(2)), for recycled carbon fuels under RED Art. 29a(2) and Art. 2 of the Regulation. That leaves at most 28.2 gCO2eq/MJ for the whole chain.

Almost all of the energy in an e-fuel comes from electricity, and conversion losses mean that more than one megajoule of electricity is needed per megajoule of fuel. The emissions of the electricity are therefore multiplied, not diluted.

Illustrative example, not a legal value: if a process needs 1.5 MJ of electricity per MJ of fuel, electricity at 20 gCO2eq/MJ already gives 30 gCO2eq/MJ of fuel, above the limit before processing and distribution are counted.

How electricity is counted (Part A points 5 and 6)

Electricity that is fully renewable under RED Art. 27(6) (formerly Art. 27(3), as cited in the Regulation) counts as zero emissions. Other grid electricity is counted, per calendar year, in one of three ways:

  • (a) with the average emission intensity of the country or bidding zone (Part C of the Annex);
  • (b) with zero if the full-load hours fall within the hours in which renewables or nuclear set the price, otherwise with 183 gCO2eq/MJ;
  • (c) with the value of the marginal unit in the bidding zone, if the transmission system operator publishes it.

The detailed rules for renewable electricity are in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1184.

Where the carbon comes from (Part A point 10)

eu holds the total combustion emissions of the fuel in use (point 13). Captured CO2 that ends up in the fuel is not deducted there. It is credited inside ei through e ex-use, and only from these eligible sources (point 10(a)–(e)):

  • (a) CO2 from activities listed in Annex I of the ETS Directive that is subject to effective carbon pricing and is incorporated in the fuel before 2036 (before 2041 if it does not come from burning fuels for electricity generation);
  • (b) CO2 captured from the air;
  • (c) CO2 from compliant biofuels, bioliquids or biomass fuels for which no credit for carbon capture and use or storage was claimed;
  • (d) CO2 from compliant RFNBO or recycled carbon fuels;
  • (e) geological CO2 that was previously released naturally.

Not eligible: CO2 from a fuel that is burned specifically to produce CO2, and CO2 that has already received a credit elsewhere. The 2036 and 2041 dates are subject to review (point 11). The energy used for capture counts as an input.

E = ei + ep + etd + eu − eccs  (Annex Part A point 1)
ei = ei elastic + ei rigid − e ex-use
Savings = (EF − E) / EF, EF = 94  (Part A point 2), minimum 70%

Good to know

  • Default values and land-use change. The Annex V default values may only be used when there are no net emissions from land-use change, that is when el is zero or below.
  • Sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). Under ReFuelEU Aviation (Regulation (EU) 2023/2405), aviation biofuels must meet RED Art. 29; their savings are calculated under RED Art. 31(1), i.e. the Annex V method used in Biofuel mode, with the Art. 29(10) thresholds. Synthetic aviation fuels (e-SAF) must meet the 70% threshold of RED Art. 29a(1) – use E-fuel mode.
  • ETBE and TAEE. The renewable part of ETBE and TAEE takes the value of the ethanol production pathway used.
  • Thresholds of 70% and 80%. The thresholds in Art. 29(10)(d)–(h) apply to electricity, heating and cooling from biomass fuels, not to transport biofuels. This tool checks Art. 29(10)(a)–(c) only, which RED III left unchanged.
  • Blends. The minimum saving applies to the biofuel itself. The blend mode shows the average of a mix and is there to illustrate the difference between bio content and savings.
