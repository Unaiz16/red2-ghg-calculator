How to read the result

Bio content is not GHG savings

Bio content says where the carbon in a fuel comes from. GHG savings say how much less greenhouse gas the whole supply chain emits per megajoule than the fossil fuel comparator of 94 gCO2eq/MJ. These are two different questions.

Growing or collecting the feedstock, processing it and transporting the fuel all cause emissions. So a fuel that is 99% bio by energy, made at 20 gCO2eq/MJ and blended with 1% fossil, comes out at 20.74 gCO2eq/MJ and saves 77.9%, not 99%. The blend mode above starts with exactly this example.

The feedstock matters most. With Annex V default values, biodiesel from waste cooking oil saves 84%, while biodiesel from rape seed saves 47%, mainly because a waste carries no cultivation emissions.

E = eec + el + ep + etd + eu − esca − eccs − eccr
Savings = (94 − E) / 94

Annex V Part C point 1(a) and point 19 (fossil fuel comparator EF(t) = 94 gCO2eq/MJ).

What each term means

E-fuels: why grid electricity can fail the 70% minimum

E-fuels here means renewable fuels of non-biological origin (RFNBO), such as hydrogen from electrolysis and fuels made from it, and recycled carbon fuels. Their emissions are calculated with the method of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1185, and they must save at least 70% against the same fossil comparator of 94 gCO2eq/MJ. That leaves at most 28.2 gCO2eq/MJ for the whole chain.

Almost all of the energy in an e-fuel comes from electricity, and conversion losses mean that more than one megajoule of electricity is needed per megajoule of fuel. The emissions of the electricity are therefore multiplied, not diluted. An illustration with round numbers: if a process needs 1.5 MJ of electricity per MJ of fuel, electricity at 20 gCO2eq/MJ already gives 30 gCO2eq/MJ of fuel, above the limit before processing and transport are counted. Grid electricity with a noticeable share of fossil generation is far above that.

This is why the source of the electricity decides the result. Electricity that counts as fully renewable under the RED rules is taken to have zero emissions; other electricity is counted with its emission intensity.

E = ei + ep + etd + eu − eccs
Savings = (94 − E) / 94, minimum 70%

Good to know

  • Default values and land-use change. The Annex V default values may only be used when there are no net emissions from land-use change, that is when el is zero or below.
  • Sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). There is no separate calculation for SAF here. Bio-based SAF, for example HEFA, uses the biofuel method. Synthetic aviation fuel made from renewable electricity uses the e-fuel method.
  • ETBE and TAEE. The renewable part of ETBE and TAEE takes the value of the ethanol production pathway used.
  • Thresholds of 70% and 80%. The thresholds in Art. 29(10)(d)–(h) apply to electricity, heating and cooling from biomass fuels, not to transport biofuels. This tool checks Art. 29(10)(a)–(c) only, which RED III left unchanged.
  • Blends. The minimum saving applies to the biofuel itself. The blend mode shows the average of a mix and is there to illustrate the difference between bio content and savings.
